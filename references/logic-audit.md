# Logic audit and repair

Use this guidance when the author asks for critique, plausibility review, grilling, plot-hole detection, or when a structural checkpoint exposes a blocking inconsistency.

The goal is not to prove the outline bad. The goal is to identify the highest-leverage uncertainty, support the finding with outline evidence, and help the author choose a repair.

## Classify before criticizing

- **Contradiction** — two confirmed facts cannot both be true.
- **Explanation gap** — the event may work, but the current outline lacks the needed bridge.
- **Plausibility risk** — possible, yet a reasonable reader may not believe it without stronger conditions.
- **Genre convention** — acceptable within the project's established promise unless it breaks its own rules.
- **Aesthetic choice** — a matter of taste, emphasis, or desired effect; do not label it a logic error.
- **Accepted risk** — the author knowingly preserves the issue for a stated creative benefit.

## Audit dimensions

### Causality

- Why does this event follow from the previous one?
- Does the turn change available options?
- Would the later plot happen unchanged if this beat disappeared?
- Is coincidence creating the problem, resolving it, or both?

### Character agency and motivation

- What does the character know and want at this moment?
- Why act now, and why choose this method over a simpler one?
- Does a major decision express capability, fear, value, or change?
- Is the character becoming incompetent only to preserve the plot?

### Information

- Who knows each fact, how did they learn it, and why share or conceal it?
- Are clues available before conclusions?
- Does a reveal reinterpret evidence or introduce its own solution?
- Are viewpoint knowledge and reader knowledge accidentally conflated?

### Time and space

- Are ages, durations, travel, simultaneity, and recovery plausible under confirmed rules?
- Can intersecting lines occupy the same chronology?
- Does nonlinear presentation conceal rather than repair a causal gap?

### World rules and resources

- Are powers, institutions, technology, social rules, costs, and exceptions consistent?
- Why can a powerful tool not solve every similar problem?
- Does a rule appear only when the plot needs it?

### Opposition

- What would the most competent antagonist or institution do in response?
- Does the antagonist have the information and resources their plan assumes?
- Does defeat arise from the protagonist's choices and earned conditions rather than opponent collapse?

### Stakes, escalation, and payoff

- What becomes worse, narrower, more personal, or more irreversible?
- Do secondary lines affect the climax?
- Does the ending answer the questions promised by the opening?
- Are setups paid off, intentionally deferred, or forgotten?

## Pressure levels

- **Light** — report only clear contradictions and blocking gaps.
- **Standard** — default; report at most three highest-priority issues with repairs.
- **Extreme** — when explicitly requested, test smartest alternatives, adversarial responses, rule exploits, coincidence load, and worst-case consequences.

Do not overwhelm the author with every minor concern. Prioritize issues whose repair changes the most downstream work.

## Issue report

For each issue, provide:

```markdown
### [Issue ID] Concise problem

**Class**: contradiction / explanation gap / plausibility risk / accepted risk
**Severity**: blocking / important / optional
**Evidence**: the specific confirmed facts or outline beats in tension
**Why it matters**: likely effect on causality, character belief, tension, or payoff
**Key question**: the smallest decision needed from the author

**Repair options**
- A. Minimal explanation or setup — effect and tradeoff
- B. Adjust motive, condition, or rule — effect and tradeoff
- C. Reorder, merge, remove, or redesign beats — effect and tradeoff
- D. Custom solution or consciously accept the risk

**Recommendation**: one option, justified by confirmed goals
**Affected artifacts**: characters, lines, scenes, timeline, clues, ending, and files
```

Use options that genuinely solve the stated issue. Do not disguise one preferred answer as several cosmetic variants.

## Repair ladder

Prefer, in order:

1. clarify an existing fact;
2. add proportionate setup or consequence;
3. adjust motivation, knowledge, timing, resource, or cost;
4. reorder events;
5. merge or remove redundant beats;
6. redesign a major line only when smaller repairs fail.

After the author chooses, restate the new canon and identify cascading effects. Offer a preview before changing multiple established artifacts. Do not silently rewrite unrelated material.

## Issue lifecycle

Track each issue as `open`, `decision-needed`, `repair-approved`, `resolved`, `deferred`, or `accepted-risk`. Preserve resolved issues and their decisions so they are not repeatedly reopened without new contradictory evidence.
